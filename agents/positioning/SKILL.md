# Positioning Skill

## Purpose

Determine how a product should be understood by a specific customer relative to the alternatives available to them, and produce defensible positioning options for human selection.

## Trigger when

- a new product or major product line is being launched
- an existing product is being repositioned
- the ICP materially changes
- competitive displacement is a strategic goal
- win rates or commercial performance indicate a positioning problem
- the current product story is failing to connect product value to buying decisions

## Do not trigger when

- the user only needs copy derived from approved positioning
- positioning is already approved and the task is message or asset production
- the issue is purely campaign execution
- there is no meaningful product or customer context yet

## Required inputs

- product truth
- target customer hypothesis
- customer problem or job
- relevant alternatives
- differentiated capabilities or product constraints
- business objective

## Optional inputs

- customer interviews
- sales calls
- win/loss data
- competitive intelligence
- market research
- pricing and packaging
- adoption / behavioral data
- existing positioning

## Evidence rules

Before making a recommendation:

1. Separate facts, observations, hypotheses, and assumptions.
2. Retrieve relevant PMM knowledge, especially positioning concepts and expert perspectives.
3. Prefer direct customer/product/market evidence over generic framework claims when making company-specific conclusions.
4. Preserve provenance for important evidence.
5. If evidence is materially insufficient, stop and identify what research is needed rather than filling gaps with plausible language.

## Procedure

1. Establish what is known and unknown.
2. Define or challenge the target customer.
3. Identify the customer's high-value problem or job.
4. Identify competitive alternatives, including non-product alternatives where relevant.
5. Inventory product capabilities and meaningful constraints.
6. Determine which capabilities create meaningful customer value.
7. Identify potential differentiated value relative to alternatives.
8. Generate multiple positioning territories.
9. Stress-test each territory against customer relevance, differentiation, proof, competition, and business objective.
10. Identify evidence supporting and weakening each territory.
11. Surface risks, tradeoffs, and unresolved questions.
12. Recommend a direction with explicit reasoning.
13. Hand the strategic choice to the human PMM.

## Positioning quality bar

A strong output should make it possible to answer:

- Who is this for?
- What meaningful problem or job does it address?
- What alternatives does this customer consider?
- What value does the product create?
- Why is that value meaningfully different or better?
- What evidence supports the claim?
- What would make the positioning fail?
- What strategic tradeoff are we making by choosing it?

Do not treat feature novelty as differentiation without evidence that the feature changes customer value or choice.

## Failure conditions

Stop and request additional research if:

- the target customer is undefined and materially affects the answer
- relevant alternatives are unknown
- differentiation is based solely on features
- the recommendation depends on unsupported assumptions
- multiple segments have materially different value drivers and the segment choice is unresolved
- product truth is incomplete or contradictory

## Human decision gate

The agent must not silently finalize strategic positioning. It should recommend a direction, show the evidence and tradeoffs, and explicitly identify the decision the PMM needs to make.

## Output

Return a structured result containing:

- target customer
- customer problem / job
- alternatives
- product capabilities relevant to the problem
- differentiated value hypotheses
- positioning territories
- evidence for each
- recommendation
- assumptions
- risks / tradeoffs
- unresolved questions
- confidence
- human decision required

## Evaluation criteria

The skill passes when it:

- distinguishes evidence from assumptions
- identifies alternatives rather than only named competitors
- connects capabilities to customer value
- produces a small number of materially different strategic territories
- explains why the recommendation wins relative to alternatives
- exposes weak evidence and uncertainty
- preserves a human decision gate
- avoids jumping directly from product features to marketing copy
