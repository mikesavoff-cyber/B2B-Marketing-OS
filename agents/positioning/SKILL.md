# Positioning Skill

## Purpose
Determine how a product should be understood by a specific customer relative to the alternatives available to them, and produce defensible positioning options for human selection.

## Trigger when
- a new product/major product line is launched
- an existing product is repositioned
- the ICP materially changes
- competitive displacement is a strategic goal
- win rates or commercial performance indicate a positioning problem

## Do not trigger when
- the user only needs copy from approved positioning
- positioning is already approved and the task is message/asset production
- the issue is purely campaign execution
- meaningful product/customer context is absent

## Required inputs
- product truth
- target customer hypothesis
- customer problem/job
- relevant alternatives
- differentiated capabilities or constraints
- business objective

## Optional inputs
- customer interviews, sales calls, win/loss, competitive intelligence, market research, pricing/packaging, behavioral data, existing positioning

## Evidence rules
1. Separate facts, observations, hypotheses and assumptions.
2. Retrieve relevant PMM knowledge and expert perspectives.
3. Prefer direct customer/product/market evidence for company-specific conclusions.
4. Preserve provenance for important evidence.
5. If evidence is materially insufficient, identify required research instead of filling gaps with plausible language.

## Procedure
1. Establish what is known and unknown.
2. Define or challenge the target customer.
3. Identify the customer's high-value problem/job.
4. Identify competitive alternatives, including non-product alternatives where relevant.
5. Inventory relevant capabilities and constraints.
6. Determine which capabilities create meaningful value.
7. Identify potential differentiated value relative to alternatives.
8. Generate multiple positioning territories.
9. Stress-test territories against customer relevance, differentiation, proof, competition and business objective.
10. Identify evidence supporting and weakening each.
11. Surface risks, tradeoffs and unresolved questions.
12. Recommend a direction with explicit reasoning.
13. Hand the strategic choice to the human PMM.

## Quality bar
A strong output answers who this is for, what problem/job it addresses, what alternatives the customer considers, what value is created, why that value is meaningfully different, what evidence supports it, what could make it fail, and what strategic tradeoff is being made.

Do not treat feature novelty as differentiation without evidence that the feature changes customer value or choice.

## Failure conditions
Stop and request research when the target customer or relevant alternatives are unknown, differentiation is feature-only, recommendations depend on unsupported assumptions, materially different segments remain unresolved, or product truth is incomplete/contradictory.

## Human decision gate
Do not silently finalize strategic positioning. Recommend a direction, show evidence and tradeoffs, and identify the PMM decision.

## Output
Return: target customer; problem/job; alternatives; relevant capabilities; differentiated value hypotheses; positioning territories; evidence; recommendation; assumptions; risks/tradeoffs; unresolved questions; confidence; human decision required.

## Evaluation criteria
The skill should distinguish evidence from assumptions, identify alternatives rather than only named competitors, connect capabilities to value, produce materially different territories, explain competitive/commercial implications, expose uncertainty, preserve the human gate, and avoid jumping from features to copy.
