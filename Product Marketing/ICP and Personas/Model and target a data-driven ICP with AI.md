### **Exercise: Build your ICP signal map**

### **Step 1:**

* Pull two lists: 10–20 best customers and 10–20 data qualified leads.

### **Step 2:**

* Enrich both with basic attributes (industry, size, sales motion, tech stack, hiring).  
* Capture at least:  
  * Company size  
  * Industry and sub-industry  
  * Markets served  
  * Sales motion (sales-led, PLG, hybrid)  
  * Team structure (AEs, SDRs, RevOps, Security, etc.)  
  * Tech stack (CRM, MAP, relevant category tools)  
  * Growth indicators (hiring velocity, expansion, new product lines)

### **Step 3:**

* Identify shared traits in your best customers (positive signals).  
  * These become “better if…” signals.

### **Step 4:**

* Identify shared traits in your data qualified leads (negative signals).  
  * These become “avoid if…” signals.

### **Step 5:**

* Draft your ICP using Eric’s structure (best for / situations / priorities / struggles).  
* **Our offering is best for**  
  * \[General type of companies\]  
* **Who are**  
  * •\[In specific situations or motions\]  
* **They focus on**  
  * \[Outcomes or priorities your product improves\]  
* **They struggle with**  
  * \[Problems you uniquely solve\]

This becomes your qualitative ICP definition.

### **Step 6:**

* Create a table of positive and negative signals. This becomes the base of your scoring model.  
* Create two columns:  
  * **Positive signals (“better if…”)**  
  * **Negative signals (“avoid if…”)**  
* This table is the raw material for:  
  * your future 0–100 scoring model  
  * SAM mapping  
  * GTM prioritization  
  * account engagement tiers (A/B/C/D)

### **Exercise: Map your engagement tiers**

### **Step 1:**

* From the previous exercise, select:  
  * 3–5 A-tier segments  
  * 3–5 B-tier segments  
  * Your C-tier and D-tier buckets  
* These will anchor your engagement tiers.

### **Step 2:**

* Assign the appropriate level of GTM effort to each tier (Passive / Targeted / Full Activation).  
* Use Eric’s tiering logic:  
  * **A-tier:**  
    * Full GTM activation  
    * 1:1 or 1:few ABM  
    * Sales \+ Marketing \+ Partnerships  
    * High CAC appropriate for high ACV  
  * **B-tier:**  
    * Targeted campaigns  
    * Signal-qualified outreach  
    * 1:few or vertical-led plays  
    * Medium CAC  
  * **C-tier:**  
    * Passive acquisition only  
    * SEO, inbound, PLG, nurture  
    * No targeted spend  
  * **D-tier:**  
    * Inbound only  
    * Zero paid, zero outbound, zero ABM

### **Step 3:**

* For Tier A and B, define one “1 Message / 1 Audience / 1 Offer” play you could run this quarter.  
  * Use the “1 Message / 1 Audience / 1 Offer” structure.  
    * **Audience:**  
      * Choose one ICP segment inside A or B.  
    * **Message:**  
      * A specific pain or scenario unique to that audience.  
  * Example from Thoropass: “Experiencing death by audits?”  
    * **Offer:**  
      * A next step that directly solves the pain (framework audit, benchmarking report, teardown).  
  * Define:  
    * Channels you’ll use  
    * Which teams participate  
    * Expected CAC level  
    * Desired outcome (SQLs, first meeting, pipeline amount)

### **Step 4:**

* Note which tiers should receive zero high-CAC spend to eliminate waste.  
* For C and D tiers:  
  * List channels, plays, and reps currently wasting time  
    * Mark everything that should be paused permanently  
    * Define what remains inbound-only  
* This is where ACV alignment typically exposes overspend.

### **Step 5:**

* Build your final tier map with engagement levels, GTM plays for tiers A and B, and zero-waste rules for tiers C and D.  
  * Your map should include:  
    * Definitions for A, B, C, D  
    * GTM effort per tier  
    * Specific GTM plays for A and B  
    * Spend elimination rules for C and D  
    * Notes on CAC-to-ACV alignment

### **Exercise: Build your full account measurement system**

### **Step 1: Prepare your ICP scoring data**

* Collect:  
  * All accounts in your TAM/SAM  
  * Each account’s ICP Fit score  
  * Current opportunity stage or last engagement date  
* Split them into:  
  * A-tier  
  * B-tier  
  * C-tier  
  * D-tier  
* This becomes the backbone of all reporting.

### **Step 2: Build your Pipeline Weather Report structure**

* Create four sections:  
* **Intro**  
  * 2–3 sentences summarizing directionally what happened this week.  
* **Weather Forecast**  
  * Label each ICP tier (A and B) as:  
    * **Sunny** (ahead of pace)  
    * **Cloudy** (stable but needs attention)  
    * **Rainy** (pipeline at risk)  
* **Leading indicators**  
  * Track week-over-week changes in:  
    * Meetings booked  
    * Accounts showing intent or engagement  
    * Accounts moving stages  
* **Lagging indicators**  
  *  Track:  
    * Opportunities created  
    * Pipeline value  
    * Closed-won outcomes  
  * Add simple sparklines or arrows to show movement.

### **Step 3: Build your TAM penetration view**

Using your ICP scores:

* Count how many A, B, C, D accounts exist in your TAM  
* Count how many have been:  
  * Engaged  
  * Qualified  
  * Created as opportunities  
  * Closed

### **Step 4: Add qualitative insights**

Each week, record:

* 2–3 accounts your GTM team broke into  
* Write a 3–4 sentence summary of what changed and why.  
* A deal story or call snippet  
* Risks emerging in key segments  
* Wins worth spotlighting

### **Step 5: Create your executive slide**

Build a 1-slide view showing:

* TAM by ICP tier  
* Penetration by tier  
* Pipeline created this quarter  
* Weather snapshot  
* One-sentence explanation of what’s improved

This becomes your monthly presentation to leadership and board stakeholders.
