# MOAT framework

"The MOAT framework doesn't tell you if you should be product-led. It tells you when and how."

Four questions:
- **M — Market Strategy.** Which market are you going after?
- **O — Ocean Conditions.** Are you in a red or blue ocean?
- **A — Adoption.** How easily can someone experience your product's value on their own?
- **T — Touch-to-Value.** Does getting users to value require your team?

"Green means you're ready now. Yellow means you can move forward, but you need to address something along the way. Red means you're not ready yet, and there's work to do before PLG will perform."

## M — Market Strategy
- **SMB:** "PLG is your natural motion. These buyers expect to try before they buy."
- **Mid-market:** "PLG is still a strong fit, but you will likely need a hybrid motion. The product drives adoption, education, and qualification. Sales steps in when deal complexity or contract size justifies the human touch."
- **Enterprise:** "PLG is not your primary motion... product-led elements... can support expansion, land-and-expand strategies, and smaller accounts."

| Flag | Condition |
|---|---|
| Green | SMB or mid-market. PLG fits your market. |
| Yellow | Mid-market with significant enterprise. PLG works, but adapt it into a hybrid motion. |
| Red | Pure enterprise. PLG is not your primary growth engine. |

"If you are purely enterprise, that single signal is enough to pause... This one is structural."

**Correction vs `ICP Definition Builder.md`:** mid-market alone is Green, not Yellow.

## O — Ocean Conditions
- Red ocean = "outperform their rivals to grab a greater share of existing demand." "In a red ocean, prospects already know how your product can help them. A product-led model is your advantage."
- Blue ocean = "create demand"; "you need to educate your market on why your new approach is better." Exception: "If your product can teach users what is possible through direct use and deliver value quickly, you can still lead with PLG in a blue ocean" (Notion, ChatGPT).
- "Blue oceans... all become red oceans eventually."

| Flag | Condition |
|---|---|
| Green | Red ocean. Buyers already understand the problem. |
| Yellow | Blue ocean. Educate the market first; plan for PLG now. |

"There is no red flag here." "A blue ocean combined with high adoption friction is a different story."

## A — Adoption
"How many hurdles does a user need to get right in order to see value in my product?"
- **Product adoption:** sign up, navigate, complete a meaningful action without hand-holding. "Table stakes."
- **Knowledge adoption:** "Not how to click the buttons, but what they need to know to get results."
- **Skill adoption:** "The difference between 'learn where to click' and 'learn how to think differently.'"

| Flag | Condition |
|---|---|
| Green | Only needs to learn the product (Notion, Slack, Zoom). |
| Yellow | Product plus real knowledge or a real skill. Needs guided onboarding, in-app education, or human support at the right moments. |
| Red | Everything: heavy product, knowledge, and skills (Photoshop). Plan a hybrid motion. |

**Yellow flag trap:** founders "forget that they have years of context that their users don't."
**Compounding:** "a yellow on adoption combined with a yellow on ocean conditions means you are asking users to educate themselves about a new category and develop new skills at the same time, which is hard to scale."

## T — Touch-to-Value
"Adoption measures how difficult it is for users to succeed. Touch-to-Value measures how much of your organization's involvement is required to get them there."
- **No-touch:** Canva, Grammarly.
- **Low-touch:** "Automated onboarding handles most of it. Your team steps in for edge cases, expansion, or high-value accounts." (Slack)
- **High-touch:** CSM, implementation specialist, or sales engineer must be involved before value. "It's not a permanent state. It's a learning strategy that you deliberately work to eliminate over time." (Helply)

| Flag | Condition |
|---|---|
| Green | No-touch. Widest PLG funnel. |
| Yellow | Low-touch or high-touch, with a plan to reduce touch over time. |
| Red | High-touch: team must be involved before users see any value. "Entirely in your control... It's fixable." |

## Reading the flags
- **All green:** PLG makes sense now.
- **One or more yellows:** PLG can work. Yellow on Ocean → pair education with product. Yellow on Adoption → guided onboarding or AI agents. Yellow on Touch → automate the path to value. "If your yellows are stacking up, slow down and build the foundation first. Fix the flags, then build the motion."
- **Enterprise only:** PLG probably not the right path; use product-led elements to support sales and expansion.

# PLG spectrum (Chapter 4)
- **Sales-led:** complex enterprise, ACV above ~$50,000.
- **Hybrid product-led sales:** "most common destination... often spanning $5,000 to $50,000 in ACV." Product leads early engagement; Sales steps in "based on user behavior, intent, and complexity."
- **Product-led / self-serve:** users convert on their own.
- "To get to your first $1 million, you'll want to choose between sales-led or product-led before layering on a hybrid motion."

# PQL (Chapter 2)
"A PQL is a lead who has experienced meaningful value using your product through a free trial or freemium model. For example, at Slack, a PQL is an account that has reached its 2,000 message limit. Using PQLs maximizes the Sales team's time because they're only reaching out to users who are best positioned for an upgrade or upsell."
