# Stage 4: Positioning story

Goal: turn the approved positioning into a narrative that makes staying with the status quo
feel risky. This is Dell's Market of One method, built on the Stage 3 statement. It does not
replace the statement.

## Read first

1. Dell L1: the eight elements and why story beats a feature list.
2. Dell L5 (`...Change, Stakes & Villain.md`): three tests for change (real, relevant, risky),
   stakes (winners and losers), villain criteria, and how demand type changes emphasis.
3. Dell L6 (`...Promised Land & ID Your Superpowers, and Harvest Compelling Proof.md`): five
   elements of a promised land, superpower selection, proof types.
4. Dell L7 (`...Memorable Simple Promise.md`): six elements of a simple promise, the Slack and
   VMware examples, the Drift page walkthrough.
5. Dunford §6-7: the pitch setup (Insight, Alternative Approaches, Perfect World) and how it
   maps to the canvas. Use it to check the story against the canvas.
6. Fletch §14 (old way vs. new way sequence) and §8 (anti-fluff test).
7. `conflicts.md` entries 1, 2, 6, 9. `crosswalk.md` demand-type emphasis rule.

## Do

Work from the Stage 1 language bank. Every element cites the interview answers or public
evidence it came from, or is marked **[Hypothesis]**.

1. **Change and stakes.** A change that has already happened. Test it against real, relevant,
   and risky (L5). Name the winners and losers.
2. **Villain.** The thing, not the feeling. Common to all best customers. Consistent with the
   Stage 2 comparator (conflict 9). Start from the ICP's Villain field and confirm or replace
   it.
3. **Promised land.** Test against the five elements in L6. It's what the customer becomes,
   not the product.
4. **Superpowers.** Features where unique, loved, and effective overlap (L6). Map each to a
   Stage 3 value theme.
5. **Proof.** At least three proof points (L6), each tied to the value it proves. Peer and
   influencer proof preferred. Proof must match the claimed value (Dunford §6 component 6).
6. **Simple promise.** Draft 3-5 options (L7). Score each on the six elements. Each option
   names the proof that backs it (conflict 6).
7. **Emphasis.** Set by demand type (crosswalk). New concept leads with change. New paradigm
   leads with the villain.
8. **Consistency check.** Map the story onto Dunford's pitch setup: change maps to Insight,
   villain to Alternative Approaches, promised land to Perfect World. If the story argues
   against something other than the Stage 2 comparator, fix the story.

## Checkpoint with Mike (checkpoint 3)

In chat: the story in four lines and the promise options with a recommendation. Mike may pick two
to test against each other.

## Output

Element tests and promise scoring go in the working notes. The constitution gets only the four
story lines and 3 promise options, each with one line on why or the trade-off.
