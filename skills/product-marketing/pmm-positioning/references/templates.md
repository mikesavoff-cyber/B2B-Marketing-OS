## Constitution

*Shape approved on the Skillvue run, 2026-09-27 (v2), with Mike's fixes to "current ways" and
"why we win" applied.* This is the only file that goes to `competitor-profiles/`. It carries no
gates, citations, interview guides, stage headers, or scoring tables. About 150 lines at most.

```markdown
# [Company] positioning

**Version [n], [date]. Status: [hypothesis until tested with customers / tested / approved].**
Built from: [inputs, one line]. Sources: (1) observed, (2) company's own claims, (3) judgment.

## The bet
[2-3 sentences. What we are, who it's for, what we lead with. What we refuse to lead with, and why.]

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| **For** | [primary audience, one line] |
| **Category** | [the frame] |

| **Villain** | [the thing] |
| **Promise** | [recommended simple promise] |
| **Proof** | [3-5 names] |

## Who it's for, and who it isn't
**For:** [who, the trigger that puts them in market, the buyer who owns the question]
**Not for:** [2-4 bad fits, each with the reason]

## What we are: the category
[2-3 sentences on why this product is hard or easy to frame.]

|---|---|---|---|

## Why we win

## Positioning statements
**Core statement:** For [primary audience] who [situation], [company] is the [category] that

differentiator.
| Situation | Who | Statement | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| [situation] | [buyer] | For [who] facing [situation], [company] [does what]. Unlike [its specific current way], [why better] | [named proof, or "not yet public"] |

- **Change:** [the shift that already happened]
- **Stakes:** [who wins, who loses]
- **Villain:** [the thing, not the feeling]
- **Promised land:** [what the customer becomes]

**Simple promise options, for testing:**

3. "[option]" [trade-off, one line]

## We will not position as
- [frame, and the shelf it would put us on]

## Open questions
- **[Question]** Resolved by: [specific evidence]

## Hands off to

- **storytelling-sales-narratives:** the story and the promise options.
- **product-launch:** [lead theme].
- **pricing-packaging:** [what the category and bundling imply].
```

## Working notes

Saved to `~/competitor-research-work/<company>-positioning-<date>/notes.md`. Cite sources here.
This is where the checkpoints are argued before Mike sees a summary in chat.

```markdown
# [Company] positioning notes, [date]

## Gate check
Inputs present / missing, and the option taken.

## Stage 1: Evidence
- Readiness (Dunford §2) and positioning problem (Dell L1), one line each.
- Primary audience: from icp-definition, or [Hypothesis] with why.
- Situations the product serves: [list, from segment map, site, and insider notes]
- Current ways, per situation: | Situation | Method | Who | Why it breaks | Evidence |
- Language bank (Dell L3): | Element | Quote | Source |
- Insider knowledge: | Claim | Who said it | Implication |
- Evidence gaps: | Gap | Who can fill it |
- Interview guide (Dell L3's 12 questions), only if interviews are missing.

## Stage 2: Strategic choice
Classification (crosswalk row), mismatches, category existence test, comparators, awareness,
game, credibility ceiling, horizontal path.
| Reading | Evidence for | Cost / risk | Verdict |

## Stage 3: Core
Capabilities → so what → value (Dunford steps 2-3). Candidate differentiators and why the chosen
one wins. Category frames tested. Statement drafts.
Gates: MKT1 Complete §4 Step 3.5 signs, name-swap per statement, Dunford §9 checks, Fletch §17, Fletch §20.

## Stage 4: Story
Element tests (real, relevant, risky; villain criteria; promised-land elements).
| Promise option | Simple | Transformation | Slays villain | Feels true + proof | Customer language | Fits demand type |
Pitch-setup check (Dunford §7).

## Stage 5: Test
Pitch storyboard, test plan, fail criteria set before testing, results log (never invented),
rollout order.
```
