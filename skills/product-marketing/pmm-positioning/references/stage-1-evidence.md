# Stage 1: Evidence

Goal: know who the primary audience is, which decisions or situations the product serves, the
specific current way each situation is handled today, and how customers and insiders describe it.
Nothing here goes into the constitution directly. Everything goes into the working notes.

## Read first

1. Dell L1 (`Transcript - Positioning - The 8 Elements of Your Positioning Story.md`): the eight
   elements and the five "do you have a positioning problem" questions.
2. Dunford §2 and §4 "Before you start": the readiness check and the preparation list.
3. Dell L2 (`...How to Identify Your Best Customers.md`): only when there's no ICP output.
4. Dell L3 (`...How to Mine Your Best Customers for Effective Positioning.md`): the 12
   questions and how to run the interviews.
5. Dunford §3 component 1 and §4 step 1: how to list real alternatives.
6. `MKT1 - Complete Marketing Framework.md` §4 Step 1: the three research outputs positioning needs.

## Do

1. **Readiness.** Answer Dell's five questions and Dunford's readiness items. No traction and no
   sales motion means a positioning thesis, not final positioning (Dunford §2).
2. **Primary audience.** Take it from `icp-definition`. If there's no ICP output, stop and offer to
   run it. Never choose the audience by where the case studies sit.
3. **Situations.** List every decision, problem, or use case the product serves. Sources: the
   segment map, the product's own solution pages, and insider notes. Group them into 3-7
   situations. Do not collapse a broad product into its most visible use case. The Skillvue run
   first framed it as hiring and promotion; the product informs hiring, reskilling, promotion,
   mobility, performance, project resourcing, and restructuring.
4. **Current ways, per situation.** For each situation, name how it's actually done today. Use
   these four groups as a checklist, then get specific inside each:
   - **Gut feel:** whose judgment, in which meeting, on what basis.
   - **Spreadsheets and manual processes:** which spreadsheet, who maintains it, how often.
   - **Competing solutions:** the named vendors from `competitor-research`, and what they do.
   - **Outdated alternatives:** the old method still in use (a consultancy, an annual ritual, a
     legacy module), named.

   Each entry says who does it and why it breaks for this buyer. Specificity test: if the row
   would fit another company's positioning unchanged, rewrite it. "Gut feel" alone fails.
   "Store managers nominate successors in the annual review, with no shared criteria" passes.
5. **Insider knowledge.** Read any founder, product, sales, or CS transcripts Mike provides. Pull
   what the company believes its differentiator is, where the money is, and what it plans to
   stop selling or give away. These can overturn public evidence. In the Skillvue run, the Head
   of Product said recruiting may become a free module, which removed hiring as the lead.
6. **Customer language.** Build or reuse an interview bank from Dell's 12 questions. Target 7-10
   interviews with best customers (L3). Sort answers for repeated words.
7. **Gaps.** What's missing, and who can fill it.

If there are no interviews, do not simulate them. Put the interview guide in the notes and mark
every customer-language field **[Hypothesis: no interviews]**. Public case-study quotes count as
class 1 if quoted directly.

No checkpoint here. Stage 1 findings go to Mike together with the Stage 2 readings.
