# Stage 2: Strategic choice

Goal: make the bet. Which competitive context do we choose, what do we compare ourselves to,
how aware is the buyer, and what do we have the credibility to claim today? This is the
largest judgment call in the job. MKT1 calls it "the most skipped over part of the entire
positioning process."

## Read first

1. Fletch §1: three positioning strategies, with the choose-when conditions for each.
2. MKT1 guide Step 2, including its "new way" section: product types, comparators,
   selection rules, the new-way trap.
3. Dell L4 (`...How to Identify Your Demand Type.md`): the nine demand-type questions.
4. Fletch §13: choosing alternatives is a decision. Two at early stage. Fletch §3 when the
   product's features match competitors': differentiation then lives in who you target and
   what you replace.
5. Fletch §19: stand-out game vs. education game.
6. Fletch §20: levels of momentum and the credibility question.
7. When relevant: Fletch §14 and §18 (category creation and budget), Fletch §16 (horizontal
   products), Fletch §15 (outgrown positioning, for Case C).
8. `crosswalk.md` and `conflicts.md` entries 2, 3, 4.

## Do

1. **Classify.** Run Dell's nine questions and MKT1's product-type table. Record both answers
   and the crosswalk row. If they disagree, that's a mismatch flag for Mike.
2. **Category strategy.** Test each of Fletch's three strategies against its choose-when
   conditions. Run the category existence test (Fletch §1). For a new category, answer the
   budget question from Fletch §18: which existing line item pays for this?
3. **Comparator.** From the Stage 1 alternatives list, choose one or two (Fletch §13), using
   the product type's natural comparator (MKT1). Name the alternatives you are deliberately
   not positioning against, and why.
4. **Awareness level.** Problem aware? Solution aware? (MKT1 table.) This sets how the "what
   is it" line gets written in Stage 3.
5. **Game.** Stand-out or education (Fletch §19). Name what each would require, from §19's
   lists, so Mike sees the cost of the choice.
6. **Credibility ceiling.** Answer Fletch §20's question: "What credibility do I actually have
   right now that makes this claim defensible?" Name the level (narrow, bigger, full) and the
   proof that supports it.
7. **Horizontal check.** If the product serves many markets, pick one of Fletch §16's three
   paths. Default to path 3 (beachhead) unless the evidence supports path 1 or 2.

## Checkpoint with Mike (checkpoint 1)

In chat, short: the primary audience, the situations, the current ways worth fighting, and 2-3
competing readings. Each reading gets the strategy, comparator, game, credibility ceiling, the
evidence for it, and what it would cost. Recommend one. Do not start Stage 3 until Mike picks.
