# The Five Segmentation Types

Source: *Segmentation Deep Dives*. Read when choosing types (Step 2) or deciding what data to gather
(Step 3).

## 1. Demographic
"Defining your customer segments based on their individual traits."
- **Attributes:** gender, age, income, marital status, ethnicity, education level, sexual
  orientation, religion. "Each business needs to decide which of these attributes are important."
- **Benefits:** personalized messaging, products, promotions, and offers. Better ad effectiveness:
  "higher return on ad spend, lower customer acquisition cost."
- **Data sources:** purchasable databases, social platforms (LinkedIn, Facebook), public records,
  surveys.
- **Example:** BIC pens marketed to women.

## 2. Behavioral
"Grouping customers based on their past actions." Harder to pull off: "harder to get the data, and
it's more granular."
- **Attributes:**
  - Purchasing behavior: linear buy vs. an education process first
  - Occasion-based: when they buy (Christmas trees)
  - Usage: how they engage with the product
  - Benefits sought: why they buy
  - Loyalty (Harley-Davidson)
  - Buying stage: where they are in the buying process
- **Benefits:** rank users by engagement and usage. More accurate, personalized messaging.
- **Data sources:** surveys, app and product usage tools, social platforms.
- **Examples:** United MileagePlus tiers (loyalty), Guinness Clear (occasion), cart-abandonment
  emails (buying stage).

## 3. Psychographic
"Grouping customers based on internal characteristics, like their personality, their values, their
beliefs."
- **Attributes:** personality type (e.g. belongers, achievers, emulators, savers), lifestyle,
  interests, life goals, hobbies, values.
- **Benefits:** sharper messaging, more targeted products, deeper understanding of the audience.
- **Data sources:** harder to get. Some from social platforms, mostly surveys.
- **Example:** Patagonia (sustainability, outdoors).

## 4. Geographic
"Looking at your customers with regards to their physical location based on where they live or work."
- **Attributes:** country, state, city, region, climate, cultural preferences, language, type of
  environment (urban, rural, suburban).
- **Data sources:** user profile data, website traffic (IP addresses), mobile or desktop usage,
  sales records, surveys.
- **Examples:** McDonald's live-thermometer billboards. Segment's "Good morning, LA" billboard in
  San Francisco (B2B).

## 5. Firmographic + technographic
"Grouping customers, often businesses, based on company and organization attributes." "Near and dear
to the heart of many B2B marketers."
- **Firmographic attributes:** industry, location, employee count, number of locations, annual
  revenue, who they sell to (enterprise vs. startup), company structure (public vs. private).
  Example segment: "an insurance company located in the US who has more than 1,000 employees with
  over $500 million in revenue a year."
- **Technographic attributes:** "technologies used." "Often overlooked, but with modern tools is
  becoming easier to do."
- **Benefits:** structured data you can load into your CRM. Easily available and commonly
  understood.
- **Data sources:** ZoomInfo, DataFox and BuiltWith (technographic), enrichment tools (Clearbit,
  Hull).
- **Examples:** Zendesk's solutions page (enterprise / SMB / startups, plus industries). Dooly,
  which only lets you sign up with a Salesforce login: technographic segmentation built into
  acquisition.

## A public-source shortcut for B2B

A company's own solutions page "is how that business has decided to segment their customer base."
Read it first. It shows the segmentation they already use.
