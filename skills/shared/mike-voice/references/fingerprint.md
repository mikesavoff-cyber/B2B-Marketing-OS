# Fingerprint

The mechanical tells of Mike's writing, taken from the corpus. Each one has an example.
Use them as tools, not a checklist to fill. A post with every tell reads like a parody.

## Punctuation and case

- **Spaced hyphen as the dash:** "In the end - I didn't get the job." Never an em-dash.
- **Sentence case when published.** The vault drafts are lowercase. That's drafting, not style.
- **Ellipses for a beat:** "And yet… I reached the finals" · "what I was trying to figure out all morning..."
- **Parentheses carry the aside or the irony:** "(it's on their website, next to the team's feet pics)" · "(ngl - it took some time to be able to post this)" · "(while being rushed and uninspired by clients and PMs)"
- **Emojis:** rare, ironic, one per piece at most. 🤡 after a self-own. 👉 before the point. 🙂🔫.

## Openings

Open hot. The first line is a claim with stakes, not a warm-up.

- A personal stake: "I will never be the same after interviewing with PostHog."
- A number that moved: "I joined Tactiq at 300K users. Now they're over 1,000,000."
- A verdict: "If your ICP doesn't tell you your ACV, you're cooked."
- A reaction: "Leah's new article about jobs, the market, and layoffs struck a nerve so I'm talking about it."
- A universal truth, told plainly: "nothing makes a buyer's eyes spark up more than you articulating their problem better than they can."

## Moves

- **The self-own.** Admit the loss plainly, then turn it: "Joke's on both of them - I don't have a job right now." · "Classic - you get in your head too much and miss the mark because you wanted it so bad."
- **Put himself in the blame.** When Mike criticizes marketers, he includes himself: "i'm also guilty of doing that early in my career - as most marketers, i'm sure." (`why marketing is struggling so much.md`) Before he criticizes anyone else, he names his own part in the problem. Being "not neutral" is a disclosure, not self-implication.
- **Ask the question, then answer it:** "What do I do best?" · "Where to start? Obviously - chill the heck out first." · "Wanna hear the really bad part?"
- **Verdict words that open a line:** "Obviously -", "Naturally,", "Classic -", "Honestly,", "That being said -", "So".
- **"in other words -"** restates a technical idea in plain words.
- **Deflation in fragments:** "Sitting on a slide or doc. Looking pretty. Doing nothing."
- **Stacked short lines for pressure:** "high expectations / low resources / tight deadlines / pressure from leadership".
- **Vertical emphasis,** once per piece at most: "no / matter / their / role."
- **Odd, concrete metaphors:** "companies that taste like AI-generated broccoli" · "a Japanese mechanic who's optimizing the intake systems for the Skyline GT-R" · "someone pre-ordered a feeling and an outcome".
- **Wordplay deflation:** "a pain in the Behance" · "too many mcps, not enough mics" · "mo' money - mo' problems".
- **Refrain of resignation:** "but hey - here we are."
- **Name people and give credit:** Leah, Elena, James, David. Mike reacts to real people, not "a thought leader said".
- **Numbered lessons with a short verdict each** (`linkedin vs reality.md`). A closing list that ends on a split line: "2. Those long shots I thought were impossible before? / They don't feel impossible anymore."

## Vocabulary Mike uses

slop · ship around and find out · you're cooked · boring AF · unhinged · fanboying · ngl ·
chill the heck out · swing for the big leagues · plant my feet · scrappy · the craft ·
the game · tech bros · pain in the ass · shit will hit the fan · doomscrolled · rockstar humans

Profanity: mild and sparing. "dumb sh*t" is censored in published copy.

## Endings

End on a line, not a summary.

- A call to act: "go and develop an unhealthy obsession like I did."
- An invitation: "If this sounds like the PMM you're looking for - let's talk."
- A turned line: "They don't feel impossible anymore."
- A question to the reader: "Which dimension is your ICP weakest on?"

## Rhythm

Mostly one- or two-sentence paragraphs with white space between them. A long sentence sets
up the situation. A short one cuts it. Sentences run long when he's venting. Let them, then
cut hard.
